Optical True-Time-Delay Systems for Phased-Array Applications

Phased-array antennas steer radio frequency (RF) beams electronically without physically rotating an antenna dish by controlling the timing of signals arriving at each antenna element. Traditional electronic phase shifters suffer from "beam squint" across wide bandwidths, where different frequencies point in slightly different directions. Optical True-Time-Delay (TTD) solves this problem by converting RF signals into optical signals and routing the light through physical path-length delays before converting them back into electrical signals.

Working as a Photonics Research Associate at The Ohio State University in collaboration with the OSU ECE Associate Chair of Instruction (Prof. Betty Lise Anderson) and Battelle Memorial Institute researchers, I conducted hands-on experimental optics research focused on system matrices, polarization control, optical alignment, infrared detection, and electronic signal calibration.

Mechanical Prototyping & Additive Manufacturing

3. Transforming Mechanical Pawn & Precision Tolerance Fitting

Motivation: Printing a static 3D model is straightforward, but fabricating a compact multi-part mechanical assembly with sliding joints, internal springs, and locking linkages requires accounting for real-world friction, resin/filament tolerances, and surface roughness.

Process & Post-Processing: I built and refined a multi-tier transforming mechanical pawn assembly using 3D printing and FreeCAD modeling. Because raw printed parts bind along tight sliding interfaces, I performed extensive hands-on mechanical post-processing—precision sanding, dremeling internal channels, lubricating contact surfaces, and tuning spring tension—across multiple assembly iterations until the transforming mechanism expanded and locked smoothly.
